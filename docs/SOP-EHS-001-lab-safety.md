## 1. Purpose

To define the minimum safety requirements for all personnel working in, or entering, the R&D laboratories, so that work with chemicals, solvents, active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs), instruments, and laboratory equipment is performed without harm to people, product, or the environment, and in compliance with applicable regulations.

## 2. Scope

This SOP applies to:

- All R&D laboratories on site: synthesis, analytical (ADL/QC-R&D), formulation, and stability areas.
- All employees, trainees, contractors, and visitors entering any laboratory area.
- All laboratory activities including handling of chemicals, solvents, reference standards, APIs, compressed gases, and laboratory waste.

This SOP does **not** cover manufacturing/plant safety, radiation safety, or biosafety containment above BSL-1; where such work is undertaken, a specific SOP shall be prepared first.

## 4. Definitions and Abbreviations

- **PPE** — Personal Protective Equipment.
- **SDS** — Safety Data Sheet (16-section GHS format).
- **Near-miss** — An unplanned event that did not result in injury or damage but had the potential to.
- **CAPA** — Corrective and Preventive Action.
- **LEL** — Lower Explosive Limit.
- **MSDS/SDS, GHS, OEL** — as per standard EHS usage.

## 5. Procedure

### 5.1 General laboratory conduct

1. Only trained and authorized personnel shall work in the laboratory. Visitors must be escorted, sign the visitor log, and wear the PPE issued at entry.
2. Eating, drinking, chewing, smoking, applying cosmetics, and storing food are prohibited in all lab areas.
3. Mouth pipetting is prohibited. Use pipette bulbs or mechanical pipettors.
4. Working alone on hazardous operations (e.g., reactions above 1 L, pyrophoric reagents, high-pressure work) is prohibited; a second trained person must be present.
5. Keep bench tops, fume hoods, and escape routes clear. Aisles and emergency exits must never be blocked.
6. Label all containers — including transfer flasks and wash bottles — with contents, concentration, date, and owner. Unlabelled containers shall be treated as hazardous waste.
7. Long hair must be tied back; loose clothing and dangling jewellery are prohibited near rotating equipment and open flames.

### 5.2 Personal protective equipment (PPE)

1. Minimum PPE in any lab: buttoned lab coat, safety glasses, and closed footwear.
2. Chemical splash goggles and nitrile gloves when handling corrosives, solvents, or APIs; face shield for large-volume transfers of corrosives.
3. Select gloves per the chemical's SDS (e.g., nitrile is not adequate for chlorinated solvents for extended contact — use appropriate laminate gloves).
4. Respiratory protection (e.g., N95/FFP2 or cartridge respirator) only per risk assessment and after fit training; engineering controls (fume hood) are always the first choice.
5. Remove lab coats and gloves before leaving the lab, touching door handles, phones, or keyboards, or entering offices and canteen.
6. Defective PPE shall be replaced immediately; the Lab Manager maintains stock.

### 5.3 Chemical handling and storage

1. Before first use of any chemical, read its SDS. SDSs shall be accessible (hard copy folder in each lab and electronically) for every chemical on site.
2. Maintain a live chemical inventory per lab (name, CAS, quantity, location, hazard class, expiry). Reconcile at least quarterly.
3. Segregate incompatibles: acids away from bases; oxidizers away from flammables; cyanides/sulfides away from acids. Use secondary containment trays for liquids.
4. Flammable solvents: keep working quantities ≤ 5 L at bench; bulk storage only in flammable-storage cabinets or the solvent store. Ground/bond containers during transfer of ≥ 20 L.
5. All open handling of volatile, toxic, or odorous chemicals shall be done in a fume hood with sash at or below the marked working height. Verify hood airflow indicator before use.
6. Peroxide formers (e.g., ethers, THF) shall be dated on receipt and on opening, tested or discarded per the schedule in Annexure 3.
7. Highly potent APIs / cytotoxics shall be handled per a compound-specific risk assessment with containment (ventilated balance enclosure or isolator) as determined by the occupational exposure band.
8. Compressed gas cylinders shall be secured upright with chains/straps, capped when not in use, and moved only with a cylinder trolley.

### 5.4 Equipment and electrical safety

1. Use equipment only after documented training. Follow the instrument SOP.
2. Inspect cords and plugs before use; report damage; do not use extension boards as permanent wiring.
3. Heating operations (oil baths, heating mantles, muffle furnaces) must never be left unattended overnight unless fitted with over-temperature cutoff and logged in the unattended-operation register.
4. Vacuum and pressure glassware shall be inspected for star cracks before use; use shields for vacuum distillations and pressure reactions.
5. Centrifuges: balance loads; do not open until the rotor stops.

### 5.5 Laboratory waste management

1. Segregate at source: halogenated solvents, non-halogenated solvents, aqueous acidic, aqueous basic, solid contaminated waste (gloves, tips), and broken glass (puncture-proof container).
2. Waste containers shall be labelled "WASTE — <stream>", kept closed except when adding, filled to max 80%, and stored in secondary containment.
3. Never pour solvents, APIs, or reagents into the sink or drain.
4. Transfer to the central waste store per the waste disposal SOP; disposal only through authorized vendors with manifests retained.

### 5.6 Emergency preparedness and response

1. Know the location of: fire extinguishers, fire alarm call points, safety shower, eyewash, spill kit, first-aid box, and assembly point. These are shown on the lab layout in Annexure 4.
2. **Fire:** Raise the alarm, alert others, attempt extinguishing only if trained and the fire is incipient; otherwise evacuate to the assembly point. Do not use lifts.
3. **Chemical splash to eyes/skin:** Flush at eyewash/safety shower for at least 15 minutes; remove contaminated clothing; report for medical attention with the SDS.
4. **Spills:** Small spills (< 500 mL, known, low hazard) may be cleaned by trained personnel using the spill kit. Large, unknown, or highly hazardous spills: evacuate, cordon, and call EHS.
5. Eyewash stations and safety showers shall be flushed weekly and inspection-tagged; fire extinguishers inspected monthly.
6. Evacuation drills shall be conducted at least twice a year and recorded.

### 5.7 Incident and near-miss reporting

1. **All** injuries, illnesses, fires, spills, property damage, and near-misses shall be reported to the Lab Manager and EHS Officer within 24 hours (immediately for injuries) using the Incident Report format (Annexure 1) or the lab-safety application.
2. EHS shall classify the incident (near-miss / first-aid / recordable / lost-time), investigate root cause (5-Why or fishbone) within 7 working days, and raise CAPAs.
3. CAPAs shall be tracked to closure with target dates; overdue CAPAs are escalated to the Site Head monthly.
4. Incident trends shall be reviewed in the monthly Safety Committee meeting.

### 5.8 Training

1. Every new employee/contractor completes EHS induction **before** entering the lab, and job-specific safety training **before** independent work.
2. Annual refresher training is mandatory for all lab personnel: this SOP, fire safety, chemical handling, spill response, and first aid awareness.
3. Training shall be documented (attendance + evaluation) and tracked in the training/LMS system; personnel with overdue mandatory training shall not perform lab work until compliant.

### 5.9 Inspections and audits

1. Lab Managers perform a documented weekly walkthrough (housekeeping, PPE, hoods, waste, labelling) using the checklist in Annexure 2.
2. EHS performs a monthly inspection of each lab; findings are logged with responsibility and due date.
3. QA includes lab safety in the self-inspection program at least annually.
